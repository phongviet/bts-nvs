# Technical Research Report: State-of-the-Art Methods for a Dense-Interpolation Drone-Tower NVS Competition

## TL;DR
- **IBGS (arXiv 2511.14357, NeurIPS 2025) and its faster successor GADA (arXiv 2607.00595, ICML 2026) both exist and both release code** — they are the single most relevant thread for your pipeline because they *are* "3DGS + depth-warped source pixels + learned residual," which is exactly what you built by hand. Adopting IBGS's learned per-pixel aggregation (and GADA's deformable-offset alignment that boosts usable warped pixels from 33% to 79%) is the highest-leverage change for LPIPS.
- **For depth at thin structures/silhouettes, PGSR and RaDe-GS are the reliable, code-available workhorses; GOF is best for surfaces but slow; the 2026 "Geometry-Grounded GS" is the new accuracy leader but has no usable public code yet.** For your distorted-camera problem, gsplat's native 3DGUT (train directly on SIMPLE_RADIAL images, no undistortion) is production-ready but MCMC-densification-only.
- **NAFNet remains the right ~1–17M-parameter efficiency backbone for a from-scratch per-scene refiner; no small-model replacement has displaced it in NTIRE 2025/2026** (the field's frontier moved to large diffusion models that violate your no-external-data constraint). For JPEG, use **mozjpeg with trellis quantization + a metric-tuned quant table + per-image knapsack rate allocation** across your fixed byte budget.

## Key Findings

**1. arXiv ID verification.** Both IDs the user cited are REAL and correct:
- **IBGS = arXiv 2511.14357**, "IBGS: Image-Based Gaussian Splatting," Nguyen, Mao, Alvarez, Liu (ANU/NVIDIA), NeurIPS 2025. Code: github.com/HoangChuongNguyen/ibgs.
- **GADA = arXiv 2607.00595**, "GADA: Geometry-Aware Deformable Aggregation for Image-Based Gaussian Splatting," Lim, Yoon, Koo, Yoo (KAIST). The arXiv listing tags it "ICML 2026" in metadata; note one arXiv index snippet inconsistently labeled a neighboring entry "ICPR 2026 Lyon" — the paper's own HTML says ICML. Code: github.com/siw00-lim/GADA.

**2. Your hand-built pipeline is a manual version of IBGS/GADA.** IBGS augments standard 3DGS color with a learned per-pixel residual inferred from neighboring training images, computed by projecting ray–Gaussian intersection points into K source views and aggregating with a lightweight 9-layer 3×3 CNN. GADA's core insight directly addresses your pipeline's weakness: heuristic visibility/z-test checks (exactly your "occlusion z-test + photometric guard") discard most valid warped pixels; GADA replaces them with learned deformable offsets + implicit confidence weighting, recovering displaced high-frequency cues — precisely the thin-antenna/lattice-mast regime you care about.

## Details

### AREA 1 — Image-based / hybrid 3DGS methods (ranked by reported LPIPS improvement)

**#1 — GADA: Geometry-Aware Deformable Aggregation (arXiv 2607.00595, ICML 2026).**
- *What it is:* Warping-based 3DGS with an iterative (K=5) deformable-offset refinement loop (max offset σ_max=7 px) plus "Geometry-Verified View Aggregation" (adaptive per-view confidence weights instead of mean pooling). Explicitly designed to recover thin structures and high-frequency detail where geometry is uncertain.
- *Reported results (per-dataset avg, PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS):* Mip-NeRF360 **28.62 / 0.840 / 0.179**; Tanks&Temples **24.92 / 0.871 / 0.144**; Deep Blending **30.22 / 0.911 / 0.235**. Beats IBGS on all three (IBGS as re-reported by GADA: 28.29 / 24.75 / 29.94 PSNR). Lowest LPIPS on all three datasets. On Deep Blending, Octree-GS has marginally higher PSNR (30.41) and SSIM (0.913), but GADA ties for lowest LPIPS (0.235).
- *Speed:* 47 FPS vs IBGS 22 FPS (2.13× faster); ~52 min training on Mip-NeRF360 (single A100). Lower peak VRAM than IBGS. GADA reports it boosts valid warped-pixel density from IBGS's 33.01% to 79.33%.
- *Failure modes:* bounded local search (σ_max=7 px) means cues displaced beyond ~7 px cannot be retrieved; textureless regions need elastic regularization to avoid "geometric instabilities or topological tearing."
- *Integration effort into gsplat/nerfstudio:* HIGH. GADA (like IBGS) is built on the original INRIA 3DGS + PGSR CUDA rasterizer (`diff-plane-rasterization`), NOT gsplat. You would port its refinement module (PyTorch) onto your existing renders, or run its repo standalone. License: verify in repo (IBGS/PGSR lineage; see Caveats).
- *Relevance:* HIGHEST. This is your exact pipeline, done better. The deformable-offset module is the single most transferable idea.

**#2 — IBGS: Image-Based Gaussian Splatting (arXiv 2511.14357, NeurIPS 2025).**
- *Reported results (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS):* Mip-NeRF360 **28.33 / 0.837 / 0.186**; Tanks&Temples **24.84 / 0.869 / 0.148**; Deep Blending **30.12 / 0.912 / 0.237**; Shiny (specular/reflection/diffraction) huge gains, e.g. Lab scene **35.06 / 0.966 / 0.056** vs 3DGS 29.17 / 0.927 / 0.123, and a stated "at least 5.22 dB gain" on Shiny. Beats 3DGS by ≥0.64/0.56/0.59 dB on the three main benchmarks while using ≥42–62% fewer Gaussians/storage.
- *Architecture:* per-pixel PointNet-style feature extractor (two 32-dim linear layers + ReLU) + 9-layer 3×3 CNN decoder; K=4 median intersection points, S=4 candidate / M=3 visible source views, depth threshold τ=0.001. 30k iterations (photometric/normal losses off for first 7k), single RTX 4090. Exact parameter count not stated. Includes an exposure-correction strategy that mimics the nearest neighbor view's exposure — useful for outdoor drone captures with varying sky exposure.
- *Failure modes (author-stated):* "may struggle in a sparse-view setting" (fine for your dense-interpolation regime — a plus); lower rendering speed + higher runtime memory than 3DGS.
- *Code:* github.com/HoangChuongNguyen/ibgs — 32 stars, 14 commits, pretrained models on Google Drive, built on 3DGS + PGSR. Python 72.9% / CUDA 22.5% / C++ 4.2%. NO explicit LICENSE file published in the repo as of retrieval (inherits 3DGS's non-commercial research license by derivation — flag as licensing risk for any commercial use).
- *Integration effort:* HIGH (same rasterizer-fork issue as GADA). But the residual-aggregation network is a clean PyTorch module you can lift.

**#3 — SuperGaussians / TexturedGaussians (per-Gaussian appearance-from-source).** Baselines IBGS beats; TexturedGauss reaches Mip-NeRF360 LPIPS 0.186 but stores per-Gaussian texture maps (heavy storage, ~1 GB). Lower priority given IBGS dominates them on quality and storage.

**#4 — Generalizable feed-forward IBR+3DGS (pixelSplat, MVSplat, DepthSplat, H3R arXiv 2508.03118, IDESplat arXiv 2601.03824).** These predict pixel-aligned Gaussians from source views in a forward pass. NOT ideal here: they target sparse/generalizable settings and depend on cross-scene pretraining; your rules forbid per-scene external data and favor per-scene optimization. Useful only as an architectural reference for source-view feature aggregation.

**Note on diffusion hybrids (GSFixer arXiv 2508.09667 ICML 2026, SplatDiff arXiv 2502.12752, GSFix3D):** these use reference-guided video-diffusion priors to fix artifacts. They violate your "no per-scene external data / no diffusion prior" preference and require large pretrained models — EXCLUDE for competition, but GSFixer confirms the "reference-guided restoration" direction is active in 2025–2026.

### AREA 2 — Depth accuracy from Gaussian Splatting for warping

Head-to-head DTU Chamfer Distance (mm, lower better) and Tanks&Temples F1 are the standard depth/surface benchmarks:
- **PGSR (Planar-based GS, TVCG 2024/2025):** DTU CD among the best of the explicit GS surface methods; planar/unbiased depth + single- and multi-view geometric regularization + exposure compensation. Strongest multi-view-consistent depth for detailed surfaces; training >100× faster than Neuralangelo. **Most battle-tested for warping** and — critically — it is the rasterizer IBGS/GADA build on, so its depth is what those pipelines already consume.
- **RaDe-GS (Rasterizing Depth in GS, TOG 2025):** DTU CD 0.68–0.69 mm, comparable to Neuralangelo (0.61); rasterized depth+normal for general 3D Gaussians; ~17 min training. TSPE-GS (arXiv 2511.09944) adopts RaDe-GS as its base and finds it gives "better stability and coverage" on semi-transparent/complex geometry than 2DGS or PGSR — relevant for see-through lattice masts.
- **GOF (Gaussian Opacity Fields):** DTU CD 0.74 mm; strong F1 on large TNT scenes via Marching Tetrahedra, but ray-tracing depth is SLOW (~1 hr training). Best surfaces, worst speed.
- **2DGS:** geometrically clean but lower PSNR and slower than PGSR/RaDe-GS; CD ≈0.80 mm at full res.
- **Geometry-Grounded GS (arXiv 2601.17835, Jan 2026):** treats Gaussians as "stochastic solids," claims "best shape reconstruction among all GS methods" and explicitly targets fine-grained/thin structures via median-depth rendering. NO public code found (flag: paper-only). Its own limitation section admits thin/near-planar structures still need dense Marching-Tetrahedra vertices.

*For thin structures/silhouettes specifically:* the depth-discontinuity problem is where all splatting methods struggle. Among code-available methods, PGSR's multi-view regularization gives the most reliable silhouette depth; RaDe-GS is the better choice if your masts are semi-transparent/see-through. DisC-GS (arXiv 2405.15196, discontinuity-aware, Bézier-curve boundary masking) is the targeted research direction for sharp silhouettes but is less mature.

*gsplat/nerfstudio compatibility:* gsplat natively supports a 2DGS mode plus distortion/depth render modes; PGSR, RaDe-GS, and GOF ship as INRIA-3DGS-fork CUDA rasterizers (not drop-in gsplat), though nerfstudio community ports exist for 2DGS and RaDe-GS. Integration effort: MEDIUM for PGSR/RaDe-GS (separate rasterizer, but you already remap depth for warping); HIGH for GOF (speed).

### AREA 3 — 3DGUT in gsplat and distortion-native rasterizers

- **3DGUT is integrated into mainline gsplat** (`nerfstudio-project/gsplat`, `docs/3dgut.md`). Enable with `--with_ut --with_eval3d` on `simple_trainer.py mcmc`. It replaces EWA splatting with the Unscented Transform, letting you **train directly on SIMPLE_RADIAL distorted images** (set `radial_coeffs`, `tangential_coeffs`, `thin_prism_coeffs`) with no undistortion — eliminating your "remap pinhole→SIMPLE_RADIAL" step and its resampling error. Also supports fisheye and rolling shutter. Source paper: 3DGUT (arXiv 2412.12507, Wu et al., CVPR 2025, NVIDIA Toronto).
- **The MCMC-only limitation is real and confirmed by the gsplat docs verbatim:** "in gsplat we only support MCMC densification strategy for 3DGUT." You CANNOT use standard adaptive-density-control (clone/split) with 3DGUT in gsplat — you must use MCMC (fixed `cap_max` Gaussian budget, SGLD noise, relocation).
- **Dense point-cloud init + 3DGUT:** MCMC densification "reduces reliance on the initial point cloud" (it relocates dead/low-opacity Gaussians to high-opacity regions), so dense COLMAP init is less critical but NOT incompatible — set `cap_max` and seed with your dense cloud. No published report specifically pairs dense-COLMAP init + 3DGUT with quantitative gains; the Self-Calibrating GS paper (arXiv 2502.09563) does combine an invertible-ResNet distortion model with MCMC densification. Flag: this specific combination is under-documented; test empirically.
- *Quality vs undistortion:* NVIDIA reports 3DGUT achieves "comparable perceptual quality" to standard 3DGS on Mip-NeRF360 for pinhole, and per the 3DGUT paper (arXiv 2412.12507, CVPR 2025) "significantly outperforms FisheyeGS across all perceptual metrics, while using less than half the particles (1.07M vs. 0.38M)" — i.e., 3DGUT reaches better quality with only 0.38M Gaussians vs FisheyeGS's 1.07M. The key win for you is avoiding resampling/cropping losses from undistortion, plus the ability to render pinhole from distorted-trained scenes (or vice versa).
- **Alternative distortion-native rasterizers:**
  - **3DGRT (3D Gaussian Ray Tracing, NVIDIA `3dgrut` repo):** true ray tracing, supports arbitrary distortion + secondary rays (reflection/refraction); Apache-2.0. Heavier than rasterization.
  - **spirulae-splat (github.com/harry7557558/spirulae-splat):** community nerfstudio-compatible splatter; uses 3DGUT as default distortion handler + FisheyeGS as cheaper alternative; supports radial/tangential/thin-prism distortion, a unified MCMC+IGS densification strategy, and bilateral-grid/PPISP exposure correction. Trains directly on distorted images. Worth evaluating — it combines several of your needs (distortion + densification flexibility + exposure).
  - **FisheyeGS:** analytic-Jacobian fisheye; narrower (model-specific) than 3DGUT.
  - gsplat's June 2026 changelog notes ~30% faster 3DGUT MCMC path on A100 and external-distortion extensions — actively maintained. License: gsplat is Apache-2.0.

### AREA 4 — Per-scene restoration/refinement from ~100–250 pairs

- **NAFNet is still the efficiency SOTA backbone at the ~1–17M scale, and NTIRE 2025/2026 confirm it has NOT been displaced by a smaller model.** Its credentials (Chen et al., "Simple Baselines for Image Restoration," arXiv 2204.04676, ECCV 2022): NAFNet reaches "33.69 dB PSNR on GoPro (deblurring), exceeding the previous SOTA 0.38 dB with only 8.4% of its computational costs; 40.30 dB PSNR on SIDD (denoising), exceeding the previous SOTA 0.28 dB with less than half of its computational costs." NTIRE evidence that it remains a core building block: NTIRE 2025 Image Denoising 2nd place (SNUCV) combined Restormer (global) + NAFNet (local detail); the NTIRE 2025 SR restoration winner (SamsungAICamera) combined a HAT transformer + NAFNet; NTIRE 2025 RAW Restoration used NAFNet blocks ("superior performance with significantly reduced computational overhead"); NTIRE 2026 Event Deblurring used NAFNet-based encoders. The NTIRE frontier otherwise moved toward LARGE diffusion/transformer models (e.g., the NTIRE 2026 Denoising top entry used FLUX.2-klein-4B diffusion + a Restormer refiner) — unusable under your no-external-data constraint. **For a from-scratch, ~100–250-pair per-scene refiner, NAFNet (or a NAFNet + lightweight-Restormer hybrid) is the pragmatic best choice.** MambaIRv2 (used by NTIRE 2025 denoising 2nd-place SNUCV) is the one credible newer alternative at similar parameter scale — worth an A/B test.
- **Refining warped/IBR composites without diffusion:** the cleanest published formulation is IBGS/GADA's own residual-learning framework (they ARE reference-guided restoration of warped composites, trained per-scene with photometric loss — no diffusion). Beyond that, reference-guided restoration literature (TransRef arXiv 2306.11528 for reference-guided inpainting; RGB-guided dual-encoder UNet fusion à la CoReFusion) provides seam/ghost-removal architectures, but most 2025 SOTA there is diffusion-based (excluded). Your U-Net-residual-on-the-blend is already the right non-diffusion approach; GADA's deformable-offset alignment is the upgrade.
- **Flow-guided residual alignment of warped source views:**
  - **SEA-RAFT (arXiv 2405.14793, ECCV 2024 Oral)** is the recommended small flow net: per the authors it "achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on the Spring benchmark with a 3.69 endpoint-error (EPE) and a 0.36 1-pixel outlier rate (1px), representing 22.9% and 17.8% error reduction from best published results," with best KITTI/Spring cross-dataset generalization and operating "at least 2.3× faster than existing methods." Code at github.com/princeton-vl/SEA-RAFT. It is scene-agnostic pretrained → permissible under your rules.
  - **NeuFlow v2 (arXiv 2408.10161)** is even faster (10×–70× speedups, >20 FPS at 512×384 on Jetson Orin Nano) at similar accuracy — best if runtime matters.
  - Use flow to correct residual misalignment of the K=3 warped source views before/inside the refiner (a lighter-weight cousin of GADA's learned offsets). MEDIUM integration effort.

### AREA 5 — JPEG optimization at a fixed total size budget

Your metric rewards PSNR (÷50), SSIM, and (1−LPIPS-VGG). JPEG choices:
- **mozjpeg vs libjpeg-turbo:** mozjpeg adds trellis quantization (rate-distortion-optimal coefficient selection) and defaults to progressive. Per the Mozilla Research mozjpeg 2.0 announcement (July 15, 2014), trellis quantization "improves compression for both baseline and progressive JPEGs without sacrificing anything in terms of compatibility," and mozjpeg added "options to specifically tune for PSNR, PSNR-HVS-M, SSIM, and MS-SSIM metrics." Per Mozilla Hacks, optimized per-image Huffman tables alone "can save 2–5% in file size" and progressive default is "about 4% smaller than baseline JPEGs" (mozjpeg tunes for PSNR-HVS-M by default). **Pick SSIM or MS-SSIM tuning to match your metric.** License: BSD-style (libjpeg-derived), commercial-friendly. Use mozjpeg over libjpeg-turbo.
- **Perceptual quant-table tuning:** the classic Jiang et al. result (simulated-annealing SSIM-optimal tables, IEEE) gives ~11.68% size reduction at ~equal SSIM. JQF (arXiv 2008.05672) and "Modeling Image Quantization Tradeoffs" (arXiv 2112.07207) provide texture-adaptive tables. A rate-distortion-optimized universal table (arXiv 2008.00605) reports ~1.5 dB PSNR gain at 2 bpp / 25% size reduction at fixed PSNR=36 dB vs default tables. Note (arXiv 2510.10970): LPIPS-optimized quant tables show "no significant advantage" — so tune tables for SSIM/MS-SSIM, and rely on your renderer (not the JPEG table) to protect LPIPS.
- **Per-image rate allocation (knapsack over quality-size curves):** this is the highest-value JPEG move for a FIXED TOTAL budget. Build each image's quality→(size, metric) curve, then allocate bytes to equalize marginal metric-per-byte (Lagrangian/greedy knapsack) — spend more on detail-rich tower close-ups, fewer on sky/uniform regions. This is the JPEG2000 rate-control and RD-OPT (Ratnakar & Livny) paradigm; SJPEG does per-image λ-based coordinate descent on the table. Since your metric is per-image averaged, maximize the SUM of per-image scores under the total byte cap — a classic knapsack. Because PSNR enters as PSNR/50 while SSIM and (1−LPIPS) are capped at 1, the marginal value of extra bytes is typically highest for images with low current SSIM — allocate there first.
- **Practical recipe:** encode each final render with mozjpeg (trellis on, SSIM/MS-SSIM-tuned table), sweep quality ~85–98, fit the size-quality-metric curve per image, then run greedy marginal-gain allocation to hit the total budget. Jpegli (Google, arXiv 2403.18589) is an alternative encoder preferred by humans at equal size (54% preference over libjpeg-turbo/MozJPEG at ~2.8 vs 3.5–3.8 bpp) but is tuned for Butteraugli/SSIMULACRA, not your exact metric — mozjpeg with explicit SSIM tuning is the safer match.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 (highest ROI, do first):**
1. **Adopt 3DGUT in gsplat** to train directly on your SIMPLE_RADIAL images — delete the "render pinhole → remap to distorted" step and its resampling error. Accept MCMC-only densification; set `cap_max` from your dense-COLMAP cloud size and seed with it. Benchmark PSNR/LPIPS vs your current undistort-based pipeline on held-out interpolated views; adopt if LPIPS improves or is neutral (resampling removal usually helps thin edges).
2. **Switch your U-Net refiner's warped-pixel handling from heuristic z-test/photometric-guard to GADA-style learned deformable-offset alignment + confidence weighting.** GADA's own data shows this raises usable warped-pixel density from 33% → 79% and is the direct driver of thin-structure/high-frequency (hence LPIPS) gains. If porting GADA's module is too costly, approximate it with SEA-RAFT flow to pre-align the K=3 warped photos before your existing blend.

**Stage 2 (adopt the full IBGS/GADA residual head):**
3. Replace your ad-hoc "3DGS base + warp + U-Net residual" with the **IBGS learned per-pixel residual aggregation** (9-layer CNN over projected source-view features), trained per-scene with photometric + normal-consistency loss and its nearest-neighbor exposure correction. This is your pipeline formalized and validated (SOTA LPIPS on 3 benchmarks). Use GADA's deformable variant if you can afford the port. Both build on PGSR's rasterizer — so also migrate your depth source to **PGSR** (best code-available multi-view-consistent depth) or **RaDe-GS** if your masts are see-through.

**Stage 3 (refiner + JPEG polish):**
4. Keep **NAFNet** (or a NAFNet + small-Restormer hybrid, ~1–5M params) as the from-scratch per-scene refiner — still efficiency SOTA and trainable on 100–250 pairs; do NOT chase diffusion-based NTIRE winners (external-data violation). Consider MambaIRv2 as an A/B alternative.
5. **JPEG:** mozjpeg + trellis + SSIM/MS-SSIM-tuned quant table + per-image knapsack rate allocation across the total byte budget, allocating marginal bytes to low-SSIM images first.

**Benchmarks/thresholds that change the plan:**
- If 3DGUT+MCMC underperforms your dense-init ADC pipeline on held-out interpolated views by >0.3 dB PSNR or +0.01 LPIPS, revert to undistort+ADC and keep 3DGUT only for the final distorted rendering pass.
- If the GADA/IBGS port cost exceeds ~1 week, fall back to SEA-RAFT pre-alignment + your existing U-Net — you capture much of the alignment benefit at low effort.
- If Geometry-Grounded GS (arXiv 2601.17835) releases code before your deadline, evaluate it for thin-structure depth — it is the current accuracy leader on paper.

## Caveats
- **Licensing risk:** IBGS's public repo has NO explicit LICENSE file; it derives from INRIA 3DGS (non-commercial research license) and PGSR. GADA's license should be checked in its repo. gsplat and 3DGRT are Apache-2.0; SEA-RAFT/NeuFlow permissive; mozjpeg BSD-like. If the competition permits research-license code, IBGS/GADA are fine; verify before any commercial use.
- **arXiv metadata inconsistency:** one arXiv index page snippet labeled a neighboring entry near GADA "ICPR 2026, Lyon"; GADA's own HTML and abstract say ICML 2026. Treat venue as ICML 2026 pending confirmation.
- **Numeric discrepancies between IBGS's self-reported numbers and GADA's re-report of IBGS** (e.g., Mip-NeRF360 PSNR 28.33 vs 28.29; TNT 24.84 vs 24.75; Deep Blending 30.12 vs 29.94) reflect re-runs/normalization differences (notably LPIPS normalization conventions). Use them as approximate, not exact, deltas. Also note the two papers use non-identical baseline sets: IBGS compares against 3DGS/SuperGaussians/TexturedGaussians (+Mip-NeRF360, Instant-NGP), while GADA adds PGSR, Scaffold-GS, Mip-Splatting, Octree-GS, AbsGS, 3DGS-MCMC, Taming-3DGS.
- **Geometry-Grounded GS has no public code** as of retrieval; its "best-in-class" claim is paper-only and unverified by third parties.
- **The specific combination of dense-COLMAP init + 3DGUT** is not documented with published quantitative gains; MCMC's point-cloud-independence means the benefit of dense init may be smaller than in standard ADC 3DGS — validate empirically.
- **LPIPS-VGG in the grader:** be careful with the LPIPS input normalization convention ([-1,1] vs [0,1]) — it materially shifts scores and is a known source of cross-paper discrepancy; match the grader's exact convention when tuning and when reading the benchmark deltas above.
- **IBGS parameter count is not published** (only "nine-layer conv, 3×3 kernels" + two 32-dim linear layers); estimate empirically before budgeting.
- Your dense view-interpolation regime (test cameras 0.2–0.5× inter-frame spacing) is FAVORABLE for warping/IBR methods (dense correspondences available) — IBGS's stated sparse-view weakness does NOT apply to you, strengthening the case for the IBGS/GADA family.

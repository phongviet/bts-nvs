::: {#abstract .ltx_abstract}
###### Abstract {#abstract .ltx_title .ltx_title_abstract}

Report issue for preceding element

Although significant progress has been made in reconstructing sharp 3D
scenes from motion-blurred images, a transition to real-world
applications remains challenging. The primary obstacle stems from the
severe blur which leads to inaccuracies in the acquisition of initial
camera poses through Structure-from-Motion, a critical aspect often
overlooked by previous approaches. To address this challenge, we propose
DeblurGS, a method to optimize sharp 3D Gaussian Splatting from
motion-blurred images, even with the noisy camera pose initialization.
We restore a fine-grained sharp scene by leveraging the remarkable
reconstruction capability of 3D Gaussian Splatting. Our approach
estimates the 6-Degree-of-Freedom camera motion for each blurry
observation and synthesizes corresponding blurry renderings for the
optimization process. Furthermore, we propose Gaussian Densification
Annealing strategy to prevent the generation of inaccurate Gaussians at
erroneous locations during the early training stages when camera motion
is still imprecise. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate that our
DeblurGS achieves state-of-the-art performance in deblurring and novel
view synthesis for real-world and synthetic benchmark datasets, as well
as field-captured blurry smartphone videos.

[1 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}Introduction {#introduction .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}
-------------------------------------------

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S1.p1 .ltx_para}
3D reconstruction and photorealistic novel view synthesis are
longstanding interests for industrial applications, such as AR/VR,
autonomous driving, large scene reconstruction, and robot navigation.
Neural radiance fields
(NeRF) \[[29](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib29){.ltx_ref}\]
and its numerous following
works \[[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib3){.ltx_ref},
[14](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib14){.ltx_ref},
[45](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib45){.ltx_ref},
[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib1){.ltx_ref}\] achieve
great progress in such 3D vision tasks. The practical deployment of
NeRFs often involves cumbersome processes of capturing a number of
individual images from different viewpoints. Therefore, recording scenes
through video is a more applicable and user-friendly setting. However,
multi-view input data acquired from video capturing is usually
accompanied by camera motion blur, and the 3D reconstruction quality can
be compromised by the blurry inputs.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S1.p2 .ltx_para}
We investigate the problem of novel view synthesis from motion blurred
images. Several
works \[[24](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib24){.ltx_ref},
[42](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib42){.ltx_ref},
[27](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib27){.ltx_ref},
[23](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib23){.ltx_ref}\] attempt
to reconstruct 3D scenes from blurry multi-view images by introducing
deblur modules to NeRF's framework. Although the aforementioned works
demonstrate significant improvements in sharp reconstruction of scenes,
they rely on the assumption that the exact poses of each image are known
through the use of
Blender \[[11](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib11){.ltx_ref}\]
or a beam-splitter
camera \[[36](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib36){.ltx_ref}\].
However, this assumption is unrealistic, as Structure-from-Motion (SfM)
with blurry images estimates camera poses with large errors.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S1.p3 .ltx_para}
More recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting
(3DGS) \[[20](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}\]
has attracted attention because of its real-time and high-quality
rendering with fast training time. While original 3DGS is designed to
reconstruct 3D scenes from a set of clean images, we also find it an
attractive option for restoring sharp scenes from blurry images because
of its exceptional capability for fine-detailed reconstruction. Despite
the potential of 3DGS to capture fine-grained patterns, to the best of
our knowledge, the problem of optimizing clean 3DGS from blurry images
has not been explored yet.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S1.p4 .ltx_para}
In this paper, we present DeblurGS, the first Gaussian Splatting
framework designed to reconstruct 3D scenes from images blurred by
camera motion. The proposed DeblurGS pipeline jointly optimizes the
latent camera motion of blurry images and sharp 3D scenes represented by
Gaussian Splatting. Specifically, we reconstruct blurry images by
accumulating 3DGS rendered images following the estimated camera motion
of training views and optimize the reconstructed blurry images to
converge with input blurry images.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S1.p5 .ltx_para}
DeblurGS improves the deblurring performance beyond existing NeRF-based
deblurring approaches by leveraging the fine-detailed rendering
performance of 3DGS. Furthermore, we observe that while previous methods
perform well under the ideal condition of accurately known camera poses
for blurry images, they falter when blur-induced error disrupts the
initial camera poses from SfM. We analyze the behavior of 3DGS when
initial camera poses are inaccurate due to blur, and propose a Gaussian
Densification Annealing strategy that enables stable optimization of the
sharp 3D scene. In addition, we have found that different blurry images
can be reconstructed depending on how sub-frame camera poses are sampled
along the estimated camera motion trajectory. To achieve more accurate
blur optimization, we propose the sub-frame alignment parameters, which
control discrete sampling intervals on the continuous motion trajectory.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S1.p6 .ltx_para}
The proposed method, DeblurGS, outperforms previous methods in
deblurring across all existing multi-view deblurring datasets. In
particular, DeblurGS is the only method capable of restoring
high-quality sharp 3D scenes from the noisy camera poses obtained
through SfM on existing datasets. Furthermore, our experiments for
real-world captured videos demonstrate the effectiveness of DeblurGS for
practical applications.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

In summary, our key contributions are:

#### Image Deblurring {#image-deblurring .ltx_title .ltx_font_bold .ltx_title_paragraph}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S2.SS0.SSS0.Px1.p1 .ltx_para}
Image deblurring is one of the fundamental tasks in the image
restoration field. The conventional deep learning approach to restore
sharp images from blurry input is CNN or transformer-based supervised
learning \[[39](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib39){.ltx_ref},
[5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib5){.ltx_ref},
[43](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib43){.ltx_ref},
[40](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib40){.ltx_ref},
[50](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib50){.ltx_ref},
[22](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib22){.ltx_ref},
[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref},
[8](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib8){.ltx_ref},
[48](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib48){.ltx_ref}\].
However, these methods require a large amount of training data paired
with sharp ground truth
images \[[36](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib36){.ltx_ref},
[35](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib35){.ltx_ref},
[32](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib32){.ltx_ref},
[31](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib31){.ltx_ref}\], and the
quality of deblurring is dependent on the scale of the data. Moreover,
these methods often struggle with generalization across different
conditions due to the domain gap problem, making their performance
inconsistent in diverse real-world scenarios. In contrast, our DeblurGS
framework deviates from the traditional data-driven paradigm,
eliminating the need for a pre-trained network trained by large-scale
datasets and inherently unaffected by a domain-gap problem.

Report issue for preceding element
:::
:::

::: {#S2.SS0.SSS0.Px2 .section .ltx_paragraph}
#### NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting {#nerf-and-3d-gaussian-splatting .ltx_title .ltx_font_bold .ltx_title_paragraph}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S2.SS0.SSS0.Px2.p1 .ltx_para}
NeRF \[[29](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib29){.ltx_ref}\]
draws great attention in 3D vision fields on account of its
photo-realistic view synthesis results. The core strategy of NeRF is to
optimize neural implicit representation with a differentiable volume
rendering technique. Several follow-up approaches aim to improve the
rendering
quality \[[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib2){.ltx_ref},
[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib3){.ltx_ref},
[19](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib19){.ltx_ref},
[44](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib44){.ltx_ref},
[49](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib49){.ltx_ref}\], while
other branches of the study are committed to mitigate the time-consuming
training and rendering
speed \[[13](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib13){.ltx_ref},
[26](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib26){.ltx_ref},
[34](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib34){.ltx_ref},
[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib6){.ltx_ref},
[47](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib47){.ltx_ref},
[41](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib41){.ltx_ref},
[9](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref},
[15](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib15){.ltx_ref},
[30](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib30){.ltx_ref},
[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib7){.ltx_ref},
[38](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib38){.ltx_ref}\] of
NeRF's framework, improving the rendering speed by several orders of
magnitude. Recently,
3DGS \[[20](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}\]
enhances variants of the radiance fields model and achieves both
detailed reconstruction performance and real-time rendering speed. By
replacing NeRF's ray
marching \[[28](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib28){.ltx_ref}\]
with rasterization that is efficient and deterministic, 3DGS achieves
real-time rendering without loss of visual quality. Our DeblurGS is also
built on a 3DGS pipeline to restore fine-grained patterns of latent
sharp scenes from blurry observations and perform real-time rendering of
reconstructed scenes.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

#### 3D Reconstruction from Blur {#d-reconstruction-from-blur .ltx_title .ltx_font_bold .ltx_title_paragraph}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S2.SS0.SSS0.Px3.p1 .ltx_para}
Recently, NeRF-based deblurring approaches
\[[24](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib24){.ltx_ref},
[42](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib42){.ltx_ref},
[27](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib27){.ltx_ref},
[23](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib23){.ltx_ref}\] attempt
to reconstruct a sharp 3D scene from blurry multi-view images. The NeRF
based approaches jointly optimize the blur operation of each image with
the sharp 3D scene that explains all blurry inputs.
DeblurNeRF \[[27](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib27){.ltx_ref}\]
and
DP-NeRF \[[23](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib23){.ltx_ref}\]
adopts a 2D pixel-wise blur kernel estimator, and
BAD-NeRF\[[42](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib42){.ltx_ref}\]
and
ExBluRF \[[24](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib24){.ltx_ref}\]
directly estimate camera trajectories of each input image. Despite
impressive novel view synthesis and deblurring performance, NeRF based
methods confront challenges optimizing scenes from inaccurate initial
poses, which is a natural assumption considering SfM pipeline estimates
erroneous camera poses if blurry views are given. Our DeblurGS
establishes restoration of sharp 3D scenes from erroneous poses by
adopting our Gaussian Densification Annealing strategy.

### [3.1 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}Preliminary: 3D Gaussian Splatting {#preliminary-3d-gaussian-splatting .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S3.SS1.p1 .ltx_para}
3D Gaussian
Splatting \[[20](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}\]
characterizes a 3D scene as a set of Gaussian primitives
$G = {\{{(\mathbf{x}_{i},\Sigma_{i},c_{i},\sigma_{i})}\}}_{i = 1}^{|G|}$,
where $\mathbf{x}_{i}$, $\Sigma_{i}$, $c_{i}$, and $\sigma_{i}$ depict
3D coordinates, covariance matrix, RGB color and opacity of $i$-th
primitive, respectively. Omitting the intrinsic notation of the camera
for simplicity, we obtain a 2D projection of 3D Gaussian represented by
mean $\mathbf{x}_{i}$ and covariance $\Sigma_{i}$ given camera pose
$\mathbf{P} \in {SE{(3)}}$ by:

Report issue for preceding element

  -- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $${{\mathbf{x}_{\text{2D},i} = {{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{x}}_{i},\text{and}}}\quad{\Sigma_{\text{2D},i} = {J\mathbf{P}\Sigma_{i}\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}J^{\mathsf{T}}}}},$$      [(1)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

where $\mathbf{x}_{\text{2D},i}$ and $\Sigma_{\text{2D},i}$ are mean and
covariance of projected 2D Gaussian, repsectively, and $J$ is a Jacobian
of the affine approximation of the projective
transformation \[[52](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib52){.ltx_ref}\].
After projecting all Gaussians to image space, we calculate the opacity
$\alpha_{i} = {\sigma_{i}{\exp\left( {- {\frac{1}{2}{({\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{2D},i}})}^{\mathsf{T}}\Sigma_{\text{2D},i}^{- 1}{({\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{2D},i}})}}} \right)}}$
and obtain the pixel RGB value $\hat{c}$ at pixel $\mathbf{x}$ by
$\alpha$-blending:

Report issue for preceding element

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $${{\hat{c}{(\mathbf{x})}} = {\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{|G|}{\left( {\prod\limits_{j = 1}^{i - 1}{({1 - {\alpha_{j}{(\mathbf{x})}}})}} \right)\alpha_{i}{(\mathbf{x})}c_{i}}}}.$$      [(2)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------

The order of Gaussians for $\alpha$-blending is decided by sorting by
distance from the camera to Gaussians. Consequently, we represent the
rendering $\mathcal{R}_{G}$ of Gaussian Splatting $G$ from the pose
$\mathbf{P}$ as:

Report issue for preceding element

  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $${{\mathcal{R}_{G}{(\mathbf{P})}} = {\{{\hat{c}{(\mathbf{x})}}\mid{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{P}}\}}},$$      [(3)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

where $\mathcal{P}$ stands for a set of all pixels on the image space.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S3.SS1.p2 .ltx_para}
Given multi-view images with known camera poses, the optimization of
3DGS is done by minimizing the L1 distance between the rasterized view
$\mathcal{R}_{G}{(\mathbf{P})}$ and the ground truth image from the pose
$\mathbf{P}$. If given multi-view images are blurry caused by camera
motion, naïvely employing Gaussian Splatting training procedure results
in poor 3D reconstruction as shown in [[Fig.]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag} [1]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#S0.F1 "In DeblurGS: Gaussian Splatting for Camera Motion Blur"){.ltx_ref}
because minimization of the L1 norm hardly converges with blurry
observation. Our approach integrates auxiliary parameters that simulate
blur operation into the original 3DGS pipeline to guide the Gaussian
splattings converge to represent a sharp scene.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

![[[Figure 3]{#S3.F3.9.3.1 .ltx_text style="font-size:90%;"}: ]{.ltx_tag
.ltx_tag_figure}[Illustration of the Sub-frame Alignment Parameters.[

based on the sampling intervals of sub-frame images. Even if the latent

.ltx_font_medium}[ row]{#S3.F3.4.2.3 .ltx_text style="color:#2380DB;"}[)
does not correspond to the blurry observation of real data. We align the
sub-frames intervals by optimizing the sub-frame alignment parameters to
accumulate precise blurry image ($\mathbf{2}^{\mathbf{n}\mathbf{d}}$[
[row]{#S3.F3.4.2.2.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_bold}]{#S3.F3.4.2.2.1

### [3.2 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}Blurry View Synthesis {#blurry-view-synthesis .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S3.SS2.p1 .ltx_para}
We aim to optimize sharp Gaussian Splatting using given motion blurred
inputs. Physically, the camera motion blur is generated from the
integration of irradiance during camera
movement \[[33](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib33){.ltx_ref}\]
such as hand-shake or trembling. Therefore, the acquisition of blurry
image $\mathbf{B}$ is represented by an integration of irradiance for
the time-varying 6-DOF camera pose $\mathbf{P}_{\tau} \in {SE{(3)}}$
within the exposure time $\tau \in {\lbrack\tau_{o},\tau_{c}\rbrack}$:

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S3.SS2.p2 .ltx_para}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $${\mathbf{B} = {\int_{\tau_{o}}^{\tau_{c}}{\mathbf{I}{(\mathbf{P}_{\tau})}{d\tau}}} \approx {\frac{1}{N}{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{N}{\mathbf{I}{(\mathbf{P}_{\tau_{i}})}}}}},$$      [(4)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------

where $\mathbf{I}{(\mathbf{P}_{\tau})}$ denotes sharp image acquired
from the pose $\mathbf{P}_{\tau}$. Here, we approximate the integral
calculation as a finite sum of $N$ irradiance
$\mathbf{I}{(\mathbf{P}_{\tau_{i}})}$, where the sub-interval timestamp
$\tau_{i} = {\tau_{o} + {\frac{i - 1}{N - 1}{({\tau_{c} - \tau_{o}})}}}$.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S3.SS2.p3 .ltx_para}
We estimate the 6-DOF camera trajectory that explains motion blur to
accurately simulate the blur operation described in [[Eq.]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag} [4]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#S3.E4 "In 3.2 Blurry View Synthesis ‣ 3 Method ‣ DeblurGS: Gaussian Splatting for Camera Motion Blur"){.ltx_ref}.
Following
ExBluRF \[[24](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib24){.ltx_ref}\],
we parameterize the rigid motion of the camera with the Bézier curve in
the Lie algebra space ${\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{e}}{(3)}$. However, we
have found that even if we accurately estimate the camera trajectory
using a Bézier curve, the blurry image is not synthesized uniquely due
to differences in the sampling positions of camera poses along the
trajectory, as shown in [[Fig.]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag} [3]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#S3.F3 "In 3.1 Preliminary: 3D Gaussian Splatting ‣ 3 Method ‣ DeblurGS: Gaussian Splatting for Camera Motion Blur"){.ltx_ref}.
Therefore, we define the sub-frame alignment parameter
$\nu = {\{\nu_{i}\}}_{i = 1}^{N}$ which calibrates each camera pose
$\hat{\mathbf{P}}{(\nu_{i})}$ on the estimated trajectory to be aligned
with the latent camera poses at time $\tau_{i}$:

Report issue for preceding element

  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $${{{\hat{\mathbf{P}}{(\nu_{i})}} \triangleq \mathbf{P}_{\tau_{i}}}\mspace{21mu}{{\forall i} \in {\{ 1,2,\ldots,N\}}}}.$$      [(5)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

We apply the definition of the alignment parameter $\nu$
(Eq. [[5]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#S3.E5 "Equation 5 ‣ 3.2 Blurry View Synthesis ‣ 3 Method ‣ DeblurGS: Gaussian Splatting for Camera Motion Blur"){.ltx_ref})
to formulate motion blur image $\mathbf{B}$ using Eq. [[4]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#S3.E4 "Equation 4 ‣ 3.2 Blurry View Synthesis ‣ 3 Method ‣ DeblurGS: Gaussian Splatting for Camera Motion Blur"){.ltx_ref}:

Report issue for preceding element

  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $${\mathbf{B} \approx {\frac{1}{N}{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{N}{\mathbf{I}{(\mathbf{P}_{\tau_{i}})}}}} = {\frac{1}{N}{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{N}{\mathbf{I}{({\hat{\mathbf{P}}{(\nu_{i})}})}}}}},$$      [(6)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

where we abbreviate $\hat{\mathbf{P}}{(\nu_{i})}$ as
${\hat{\mathbf{P}}}_{i}$ for brevity. By introducing the sub-frame
alignment parameter $\nu$, we overcome the challenge of non-unique blur
synthesis from a single trajectory.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S3.SS2.p4 .ltx_para}
We synthesize a motion blurred rendering by adopting Gaussian Splatting
rasterization described in Sec. [[3.1]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#S3.SS1 "3.1 Preliminary: 3D Gaussian Splatting ‣ 3 Method ‣ DeblurGS: Gaussian Splatting for Camera Motion Blur"){.ltx_ref}
to the motion blur formulation. Given approximate 6-DOF camera motion,
we retrieve the sub-frame poses
${\{{\hat{\mathbf{P}}}_{i}\}}_{i = 1}^{N}$ corresponding to each
sub-interval ${\{\tau_{i}\}}_{i = 1}^{N}$. We generate a blurry
rendering by accumulating sharp rendering of Gaussian Splatting from the
sub-frame poses. We also apply a gamma correction function to the
synthesized blurry view to correctly replicate the image acquisition
process of the camera. When a camera converts the irradiance to a
digital image, a nonlinear function is applied to brighten the raw
image \[[12](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib12){.ltx_ref}\].
We choose to leverage the widely used gamma correction function
${\gamma{(x)}} = x^{1/2.2}$ to the synthesized blurry view, following
previous works on blur
synthesis \[[27](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib27){.ltx_ref},
[24](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib24){.ltx_ref},
[36](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib36){.ltx_ref}\].
Finally, applying the rasterization process depicted in
Eq. [[2]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#S3.E2 "Equation 2 ‣ 3.1 Preliminary: 3D Gaussian Splatting ‣ 3 Method ‣ DeblurGS: Gaussian Splatting for Camera Motion Blur"){.ltx_ref}
to the blur operation (Eq. [[6]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#S3.E6 "Equation 6 ‣ 3.2 Blurry View Synthesis ‣ 3 Method ‣ DeblurGS: Gaussian Splatting for Camera Motion Blur"){.ltx_ref}),
we formulate the generation of blurry rendering $\hat{\mathbf{B}}$ as
integration of sharp rendering
$\mathcal{R}_{G}{({\hat{\mathbf{P}}}_{i})}$ of the 3DGS scene $G$ from
the retrieved sub-frame poses ${\hat{\mathbf{P}}}_{i}$, followed by the
gamma correction function $\gamma{( \cdot )}$:

Report issue for preceding element

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $${{\hat{\mathbf{B}}{(G)}} = {\gamma\left( {\frac{1}{N}{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{N}{\mathcal{R}_{G}{({\hat{\mathbf{P}}}_{i})}}}} \right)}}.$$      [(7)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

We minimize the distance between synthesized blurry rendering
$\hat{\mathbf{B}}{(G)}$ and the blurry observation to optimize the
latent sharp scene.

### [3.3 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}Optimization from Inaccurate Poses {#optimization-from-inaccurate-poses .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S3.SS3.p1 .ltx_para}
Given a set of $M$ blurry observations
${\{\mathbf{B}_{i}\}}_{i = 1}^{M}$, our goal is to find 3DGS parameters
$G$ which represent a sharp scene. To achieve this, we define a set of
camera motion parameters (Sec. [[3.2]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#S3.SS2 "3.2 Blurry View Synthesis ‣ 3 Method ‣ DeblurGS: Gaussian Splatting for Camera Motion Blur"){.ltx_ref})
to estimate camera trajectory and alignment of sub-frames. For $i$-th
camera, we assign the camera motion parameter to retrieve sub-frame
poses ${\{{\hat{\mathbf{P}}}_{j}^{(i)}\}}_{j = 1}^{N}$ from the
estimated motion, where ${\hat{\mathbf{P}}}_{j}^{(i)}$ denotes the
$j$-th sub-frame pose of $i$-th camera. We initialize the camera
trajectory from the camera poses obtained by executing the SfM pipeline
COLMAP \[[37](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib37){.ltx_ref}\]
for all blurry observations and the $N$ alignment parameter
${\{\nu_{j}^{(i)}\}}_{j = 1}^{N}$ to be evenly spaced within the range
of $\lbrack 0,1\rbrack$. Subsequently, the optimization process adjusts
the camera trajectory and the sub-frame parameter $\nu^{(i)}$ toward
latent camera motion. We accumulate sharp renderings from each sub-frame
pose and reconstruct blurry rendering ${\hat{\mathbf{B}}}_{i}$ as
outlined in Eq. [[7]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#S3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ 3.2 Blurry View Synthesis ‣ 3 Method ‣ DeblurGS: Gaussian Splatting for Camera Motion Blur"){.ltx_ref}.

::: {#S3.SS3.SSS0.Px1 .section .ltx_paragraph}
#### Gaussian Densification Annealing {#gaussian-densification-annealing .ltx_title .ltx_font_bold .ltx_title_paragraph}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S3.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1 .ltx_para}
The initial poses obtained by COLMAP are erroneous since the
conventional feature matching algorithm is conducted on noisy features
from blurry images. Given the inaccurate initial poses, joint estimation
of camera motion leads to the generation of the Gaussians in incorrect
positions during the early stages of optimization. Specifically, if the
gradient with respect to the position of the Gaussian exceeds the
densification threshold $\theta$, the Gaussian is split into two.
Consequently, the Gaussians at incorrect positions disrupt the
optimization process by trying to fit the training images from the
incorrect locations. To prevent the generation of premature Gaussian
splitting in an incorrect location, we employ an annealing strategy of
the densification threshold $\theta$. We gradually anneal $\theta$ down
from a higher initial $\theta$, allowing for a more refined
densification when the camera motion is sufficiently optimized. The
annealing strategy for $\theta$ prioritizes the camera motion's accurate
optimization before the scene attempts to represent fine details through
densification, thereby mitigating the disruptions caused by Gaussians at
an erroneous location.

#### Reconstruction Loss {#reconstruction-loss .ltx_title .ltx_font_bold .ltx_title_paragraph}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S3.SS4.SSS0.Px1.p1 .ltx_para}
We employ a reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}}$ to converge
the blurry rendering ${\hat{\mathbf{B}}}_{i}$ to the blurry observation
$\mathbf{B}_{i}$:

Report issue for preceding element

  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $${\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} = {\sum\limits_{i}{\parallel{\mathbf{B}_{i} - {\hat{\mathbf{B}}}_{i}}\parallel}_{1}}}.$$      [(8)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

The reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}}$ leads joint
optimization of the motion trajectories, the sub-frame alignment
parameters and the sharp Gaussian Splatting.

::: {#S3.SS4.SSS0.Px2 .section .ltx_paragraph}
#### Temporal Smoothness Loss {#temporal-smoothness-loss .ltx_title .ltx_font_bold .ltx_title_paragraph}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S3.SS4.SSS0.Px2.p1 .ltx_para}
The reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}}$ minimizes the error
between the integration of sharp renderings and the corresponding blurry
observation. However, the optimization relying solely on
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}}$ can converge to unrealistic latent Gaussians
and motion trajectories due to the ill-posedness of the deblurring
problem. To regularize the ill-posedness of the blurry view synthesis
in [[Eq.]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag} [7]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#S3.E7 "In 3.2 Blurry View Synthesis ‣ 3 Method ‣ DeblurGS: Gaussian Splatting for Camera Motion Blur"){.ltx_ref},
we apply a temporal smoothness loss:

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S3.SS4.SSS0.Px2.p2 .ltx_para}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $${\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}} = {\frac{1}{N}{\sum\limits_{i,j}{\parallel{{\mathcal{R}_{G}{({\hat{\mathbf{P}}}_{j + 1}^{(i)})}} - {\mathcal{R}_{G}{({\hat{\mathbf{P}}}_{j}^{(i)})}}}\parallel}_{2}}}},$$      [(9)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}$ penalizes the RGB difference of
neighboring sub-frame renderings to prevent abrupt changes in the
rendering of adjacent viewpoints. We finalize our optimization objective
$\mathcal{L}$ by combining $\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}}$ to
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}$:

Report issue for preceding element

  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $${\mathcal{L} = {\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} + {\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}}}}.$$      [(10)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

We assign $\lambda = 0.05$, gradually annealing down to $\lambda = 0.01$
during the optimization. After the training procedure, we only keep
sharp Gaussian Splatting $G$ and all camera motion parameters are not
used in sharp view rendering at inference time.

### [4.1 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}Experimental Setup {#experimental-setup .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S4.SS1.SSS0.Px1 .section .ltx_paragraph}
#### Datasets {#datasets .ltx_title .ltx_font_bold .ltx_title_paragraph}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S4.SS1.SSS0.Px1.p1 .ltx_para}
We perform comprehensive experiments on two real-world benchmark
datasets,
Real-motion-blur \[[27](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib27){.ltx_ref}\]
and
ExBlur \[[24](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib24){.ltx_ref}\],
and also synthetic dataset Synthetic Extreme
Blur \[[24](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib24){.ltx_ref}\].
All datasets contain multiple scenes, and each scene contains 20 to 40
blurry training images with hand-shaking camera motion blur, as well as
4 to 6 sharp testing images. For the Real-motion-blur dataset, the
camera poses are obtained by
COLMAP \[[37](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib37){.ltx_ref}\]
with a set of training and testing images. For the ExBlur dataset, the
amount of blur is more severe than the Real-motion-Blur dataset. Each
blurry image in the ExBlur dataset has its sharp pair obtained from
beam-splitter \[[36](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib36){.ltx_ref}\].
The camera poses of the blurry observation are calculated from the sharp
pair in the ExBlur dataset. For the Synthetic Extreme Blur dataset also
contains more severe motion blur than the Real-motion-blur dataset, and
the camera pose is known by generating from
Blender \[[11](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib11){.ltx_ref}\].

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S4.SS1.SSS0.Px1.p2 .ltx_para}
To better evaluate sharp 3D reconstruction capabilities in realistic
scenarios, we divide the ExBlur dataset into two experimental settings
based on the accuracy of initial poses and points cloud. The first
setup, referred to as ExBlur-CleanPose or
[ExBlur-CP]{#S4.SS1.SSS0.Px1.p2.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic}, is
unchanged from the dataset's public release, which is compromised of
accurate camera pose and point clouds from COLMAP with sharp image
pairs. On the other hand, ExBlur-NoisyPose or
[ExBlur-NP]{#S4.SS1.SSS0.Px1.p2.1.2 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} setting
exclusively utilizes only blurry images for COLMAP processing, notably
excluding sharp test images as well. To obtain the camera poses and
point clouds from the inaccuracies induced by the blur, we loosen
pre-defined COLMAP hyperparameters such as the re-projection error
threshold and the number of inlier feature points. We detail the
modifications made for COLMAP with blurry images in the supplementary
materials. Given the significant blur that affects all images, not every
image is guaranteed to be registered successfully in the process of
Structure-from-Motion. However, we proceed our experiments with a
minimum of 16 images for registration, which is at least 60% of the
total images.

#### Implementation Details {#implementation-details .ltx_title .ltx_font_bold .ltx_title_paragraph}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S4.SS1.SSS0.Px2.p1 .ltx_para}
Our DeblurGS pipeline is built upon the official implementation of
3DGS \[[20](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}\].
We set the number of sub-frames for blurry rendering synthesis to
$N = 21$, the same as
ExBluRF \[[24](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib24){.ltx_ref}\]
for a fair comparison. Additionally, we use the $9$-th order Bézier
curve in our camera trajectory estimation. We use Adam
optimizer \[[21](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib21){.ltx_ref}\]
with default configuration, and all the learning rate schema regarding
the 3DGS model is unchanged from its built-in setting. For the camera
motion parameters, we schedule the learning rate of $1 \times 10^{- 2}$
for the translation part of the camera trajectory, $1 \times 10^{- 3}$
for the rotation, and $3 \times 10^{- 3}$ for the alignment parameter
$\nu$, all of which decay exponentially to be halved for every 15k
iterations. Each scene is optimized for 150k iterations using a single
NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU.

#### Evaluation Setup {#evaluation-setup .ltx_title .ltx_font_bold .ltx_title_paragraph}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S4.SS2.SSS0.Px1.p1 .ltx_para}
The performance of novel view synthesis is evaluated by measuring PSNR,
SSIM \[[17](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib17){.ltx_ref}\],
and
LPIPS \[[51](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib51){.ltx_ref}\]
between rendering and sharp test images. For all settings, since test
poses are misaligned due to the joint optimization of 6-DOF camera
motion, we perform the post-optimization process to align the test image
poses, following previous 3D reconstruction works on pose-free
settings \[[46](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib46){.ltx_ref},
[18](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib18){.ltx_ref},
[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib4){.ltx_ref},
[25](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib25){.ltx_ref},
[16](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib16){.ltx_ref}\] and
blurry-image
settings \[[42](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib42){.ltx_ref},
[24](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11358v2#bib.bib24){.ltx_ref}\]. In the
ExBlur-NP setup, we fix the training camera poses and generate sharp
renderings from these viewpoints to conduct COLMAP's image registration
and acquire poses for test images. After the test image registration, we
align the test image poses by the post-optimization process similar to
the evaluation process for the other setups. Note that the scene
parameters such as NeRF, voxels and 3DGS remain frozen during
post-optimization, and the purpose of the test-time optimization is
strictly limited to factoring out the pose misalignment of test view
images.

To evaluate the practical applicability of our DeblurGS method, we

::: {#S5 .section .ltx_section}
[5 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}Conclusion {#conclusion .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}
-----------------------------------------

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S5.p1 .ltx_para}
In this paper, we present DeblurGS, a method for reconstructing a sharp
3D scene from a collection of motion blurred images. We simulate the
camera motion to synthesize a blurry view, and optimize 3D Gaussian
Splatting by minimizing distance between given blurry observation and
generated blur. With our Gaussian Densification Annealing strategy, the
camera motion converges to the latent camera movement, even initialized
from noisy camera poses, the natural outcome of SfM from blurry
observations. The capability to optimize from imprecise poses
underscores the practicality of our framework, showcasing the successful
deblurring of videos captured with smartphones. DeblurGS outperforms all
existing methods in the sharp 3D scene reconstruction task, achieving
state-of-the-art in both experimental and practical settings.

Instructions for reporting errors
---------------------------------

We are continuing to improve HTML versions of papers, and your feedback
helps enhance accessibility and mobile support. To report errors in the
HTML that will help us improve conversion and rendering, choose any of
the methods listed below:

-   Click the \"Report Issue\" button.
-   Open a report feedback form via keyboard, use \"**Ctrl + ?**\".
-   Make a text selection and click the \"Report Issue for Selection\"
    button near your cursor.
-   You can use Alt+Y to toggle on and Alt+Shift+Y to toggle off
    accessible reporting links at each section.

Our team has already identified [the following
issues](https://github.com/arXiv/html_feedback/issues){.ltx_ref}. We
appreciate your time reviewing and reporting rendering errors we may not
have found yet. Your efforts will help us improve the HTML versions for
all readers, because disability should not be a barrier to accessing
research. Thank you for your continued support in championing open
access for all.

Have a free development cycle? Help support accessibility at arXiv! Our
collaborators at LaTeXML maintain a [list of packages that need
conversion](https://github.com/brucemiller/LaTeXML/wiki/Porting-LaTeX-packages-for-LaTeXML){.ltx_ref},
and welcome [developer
contributions](https://github.com/brucemiller/LaTeXML/issues){.ltx_ref}.

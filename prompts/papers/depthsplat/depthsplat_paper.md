::: {#abstract .ltx_abstract}
###### Abstract {#abstract .ltx_title .ltx_title_abstract}

Report issue for preceding element

Gaussian splatting and single-view depth estimation are typically
studied in isolation. In this paper, we present DepthSplat to connect
Gaussian splatting and depth estimation and study their interactions.
More specifically, we first contribute a robust multi-view depth model
by leveraging pre-trained monocular depth features, leading to
high-quality feed-forward 3D Gaussian splatting reconstructions. We also
show that Gaussian splatting can serve as an unsupervised pre-training
objective for learning powerful depth models from large-scale multi-view
posed datasets. We validate the synergy between Gaussian splatting and
depth estimation through extensive ablation and cross-task transfer
experiments. Our DepthSplat achieves state-of-the-art performance on
ScanNet, RealEstate10K and DL3DV datasets in terms of both depth
estimation and novel view synthesis, demonstrating the mutual benefits
of connecting both tasks. In addition, DepthSplat enables feed-forward
reconstruction from 12 input views ($512 \times 960$ resolutions) in 0.6
seconds.

[1 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}Introduction {#introduction .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}
-------------------------------------------

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S1.p1 .ltx_para}
Novel view synthesis \[[[4]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib4){.ltx_ref},
[[82]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib82){.ltx_ref}\]
and depth prediction \[[[44]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib44){.ltx_ref},
[[20]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}\]
are two fundamental tasks in computer vision, serving as the driving
force behind numerous applications ranging from augmented reality to
robotics and autonomous driving. There have been notable advancements in
both areas recently.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S1.p2 .ltx_para}
For novel view synthesis, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) \[[[29]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib29){.ltx_ref}\]
has emerged as a popular technique due to its impressive real-time
performance while attaining high visual fidelity. Recently, advances in
feed-forward 3DGS models \[[[7]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib7){.ltx_ref},
[[9]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref},
[[48]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib48){.ltx_ref}\]
have been introduced to alleviate the need for tedious per-scene
optimization, also enabling few-view 3D reconstruction. The
state-of-the-art sparse-view method MVSplat \[[[9]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}\]
relies on feature matching-based multi-view depth
estimation \[[[65]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib65){.ltx_ref}\]
to localize the 3D Gaussian positions, which makes it suffer from
similar limitations (*e.g.*, occlusions, texture-less regions, and
reflective surfaces) as other multi-view depth methods \[[[44]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib44){.ltx_ref},
[[71]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib71){.ltx_ref},
[[25]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib25){.ltx_ref},
[[56]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib56){.ltx_ref},
[[18]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib18){.ltx_ref}\].

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S1.p3 .ltx_para}
On the other hand, significant progress has been made in monocular depth
estimation, with recent models \[[[69]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib69){.ltx_ref},
[[28]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib28){.ltx_ref},
[[74]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib74){.ltx_ref},
[[22]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib22){.ltx_ref},
[[40]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib40){.ltx_ref},
[[19]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib19){.ltx_ref}\]
achieving robust predictions on diverse in-the-wild data. However, these
depths typically lack consistent scales across multiple views,
constraining their performance in downstream tasks like 3D
reconstruction \[[[59]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib59){.ltx_ref},
[[73]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib73){.ltx_ref}\].

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S1.p4 .ltx_para}
The integration of 3DGS with single-view depth estimation presents a
compelling solution to overcome the individual limitations of each
technique while at the same time enhancing their strengths. To this end,
we introduce *DepthSplat*, which exploits the complementary nature of
sparse-view feed-forward 3DGS and robust monocular depth estimation to
improve the performance for both tasks.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S1.p5 .ltx_para}
Specifically, we first contribute a robust multi-view depth model by
integrating pre-trained monocular depth features \[[[70]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib70){.ltx_ref}\]
to the multi-view feature matching branch, which not only maintains the
consistency of multi-view depth models but also leads to more robust
results in situations that are hard to match (*e.g*.[]{#S1.p5.1.2
.ltx_text}, occlusions, texture-less regions and reflective surfaces).
The predicted multi-view depth maps are then unprojected to 3D as the
Gaussian centers, and we use an additional lightweight network to
predict other remaining Gaussian parameters. They are combined together
to achieve novel view synthesis with the splatting
operation \[[[29]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib29){.ltx_ref}\].

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S1.p6 .ltx_para}
While previous methods \[[[1]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib1){.ltx_ref},
[[31]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib31){.ltx_ref},
[[11]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib11){.ltx_ref}\]
also try to fuse monocular and multi-view depths, they usually rely on
sophisticated architectures. In contrast, we identify the power of
[off-the-shelf]{#S1.p6.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} pre-trained
monocular depth models and propose to augment multi-view cost volumes
with monocular features, leading to a simpler model and stronger
performance.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S1.p7 .ltx_para}
Thanks to our improved multi-view depth model, the quality of novel view
synthesis with Gaussian splatting is also significantly enhanced (see
[[Fig.]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag} [1]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#S0.F1 "In DepthSplat: Connecting Gaussian Splatting and Depth"){.ltx_ref}
left). In addition, our Gaussian splatting module is fully
differentiable, which requires only photometric supervision to optimize
all model components. This provides a new, unsupervised way to pre-train
depth prediction models on large-scale multi-view posed datasets without
requiring ground truth geometry information. The pre-trained depth model
can be further fine-tuned for specific depth tasks and achieves superior
results over training from scratch (see [[Fig.]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag} [1]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#S0.F1 "In DepthSplat: Connecting Gaussian Splatting and Depth"){.ltx_ref}
right, where unsupervised pre-training leads to improved performance).

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S1.p8 .ltx_para}
We conduct extensive experiments on the large-scale
TartanAir \[[[58]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib58){.ltx_ref}\],
ScanNet \[[[14]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib14){.ltx_ref}\]
and RealEstate10K \[[[82]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib82){.ltx_ref}\]
datasets for depth estimation and Gaussian splatting tasks, as well as
the recently introduced DL3DV \[[[32]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib32){.ltx_ref}\]
dataset, which features complex real-world scenes and thus is more
challenging. Under various evaluation settings, our DepthSplat achieves
state-of-the-art results. We also achieve promising feed-forward
reconstruction results of large-scale or $360^{\circ}$ scenes from 12
input views ($512 \times 960$ resolutions) in 0.6 seconds. The strong
performance on both tasks demonstrates the mutual benefits of connecting
Gaussian splatting and depth.

[2 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}Related Work {#related-work .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#S2.p1 .ltx_para .ltx_noindent}
[Multi-View Depth Estimation]{#S2.p1.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_bold}. As a
core component of classical multi-view stereo
pipelines \[[[44]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib44){.ltx_ref}\],
multi-view depth estimation exploits multi-view photometric consistency
across multiple images to perform feature matching and predict the depth
map of the reference image. In recent years, many learning-based
methods \[[[71]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib71){.ltx_ref},
[[25]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib25){.ltx_ref},
[[56]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib56){.ltx_ref},
[[18]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib18){.ltx_ref},
[[57]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib57){.ltx_ref},
[[15]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib15){.ltx_ref},
[[6]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib6){.ltx_ref}\]
have been proposed to improve depth accuracy. Though these
learning-based methods significantly improve the depth quality compared
to traditional methods \[[[23]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib23){.ltx_ref},
[[44]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib44){.ltx_ref},
[[67]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib67){.ltx_ref}\],
they cannot handle challenging situations where the multi-view
photometric consistency assumption is not
guaranteed, *e.g*.[]{#S2.p1.1.3 .ltx_text}, occlusions, low-textured
areas, and non-Lambertian surfaces.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S2.p2 .ltx_para .ltx_noindent}
[Monocular Depth Estimation]{#S2.p2.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_bold}.
Recently, we have witnessed significant progress in depth estimation
from a single image \[[[40]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib40){.ltx_ref},
[[2]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib2){.ltx_ref},
[[74]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib74){.ltx_ref},
[[69]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib69){.ltx_ref},
[[28]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib28){.ltx_ref}\],
and existing methods can produce surprisingly accurate results on
diverse in-the-wild data. However, monocular depth methods inherently
suffer from scale ambiguities, which makes it challenging to use them
for downstream tasks like 3D reconstruction \[[[73]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib73){.ltx_ref}\].
In this paper, we leverage the powerful features from a pre-trained
monocular depth model \[[[70]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib70){.ltx_ref}\]

[3 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}DepthSplat {#depthsplat .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}
-----------------------------------------

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S3.p1 .ltx_para}
Given $N$ input images
${\{{\mathbf{I}}^{i}\}}_{i = 1}^{N},{({\mathbf{I}}^{i} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{H \times W \times 3}}$,
where $H$ and $W$ are the image sizes) with corresponding projection
matrices ${\{\mathbf{P}_{i}\}}_{i = 1}^{N}$,
($\mathbf{P}_{i} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{3 \times 4}$, computed from the
intrinsic and extrinsic matrices), our goal is to predict dense
per-pixel depth $\mathbf{D}_{i} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{H \times W}$ and
per-pixel Gaussian parameters
${\{{({\mathbf{μ}}_{j},\alpha_{j},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{j},{\mathbf{c}}_{j})}\}}_{j = 1}^{H \times W \times N}$
for each image, where ${\mathbf{μ}}_{j}$, $\alpha_{j}$,
$\mathbf{\Sigma}_{j}$ and ${\mathbf{c}}_{j}$ are the 3D Gaussian's
position, opacity, covariance, and color. As shown in [[Fig.]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag} [2]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#S2.F2 "In 2 Related Work ‣ DepthSplat: Connecting Gaussian Splatting and Depth"){.ltx_ref},
key to our method is a multi-view depth model augmented with monocular
depth features, where we obtain the position ${\mathbf{μ}}_{j}$ of each
Gaussian by unprojecting depth with camera parameters, and other
Gaussian parameters are predicted by an additional head.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S3.p2 .ltx_para}
More specifically, our depth model consists of two branches: one for
modeling feature matching using cost volumes, and another for extracting
monocular features from a pre-trained monocular depth network. The cost
volumes and monocular features are concatenated together for subsequent
depth regression with a 2D U-Net and a softmax layer. For the depth
task, we train our depth model with ground truth depth supervision. Our
full model for novel view synthesis is trained with the rendering loss,
which can also be used as an unsupervised pre-training stage for the
depth model. We introduce the individual components below.

:::

::: {#S3.SS1 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [3.1 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}Multi-View Feature Matching {#multi-view-feature-matching .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S3.SS1.p1 .ltx_para}
In this branch, we extract multi-view features with a multi-view
Transformer architecture and then build multiple cost volumes that
correspond to each input view.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S3.SS1.p2 .ltx_para .ltx_noindent}
[Multi-View Feature Extraction]{#S3.SS1.p2.10.1 .ltx_text
.ltx_font_bold}. For $N$ input images, we first use a lightweight
weight-sharing ResNet \[[[27]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib27){.ltx_ref}\]
architecture to get $s \times$ downsampled features for each image
independently. To handle different image resolutions, we make the
downsampling factor $s$ flexible by controlling the number of stride-2
$3 \times 3$ convolutions. For example, the downsampling factor $s$ is 4
if two stride-2 convolutions are used and 8 if three are used. To
exchange information across different views, we use a multi-view Swin
Transformer \[[[34]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib34){.ltx_ref},
[[64]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib64){.ltx_ref},
[[65]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib65){.ltx_ref}\]
which contains six stacked self- and cross-attention layers to obtain
multi-view-aware features ${\{{\mathbf{F}}_{i}\}}_{i = 1}^{N}$,
${\mathbf{F}}^{i} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{\frac{H}{s} \times \frac{W}{s} \times C}$,
where $C$ is the feature dimension. When more than two images ($N > 2$)
are given as input, we perform cross-attention between each reference
view and its top-$2$ nearest neighboring views, which are selected based
on their camera distances to the reference view. This makes computation
tractable with many input views.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S3.SS1.p3 .ltx_para .ltx_noindent}
[Feature Matching]{#S3.SS1.p3.15.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_bold}. We encode
the feature matching information across different views with the
plane-sweep stereo approach \[[[13]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib13){.ltx_ref},
[[65]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib65){.ltx_ref}\].
More specifically, for each view $i$, we first uniformly sample $D$
depth candidates ${\{ d_{m}\}}_{m = 1}^{D}$ from the near and far depth
ranges and then warp the feature ${\mathbf{F}}^{j}$ of view $j$ to view
$i$ with the camera projection matrix and each depth candidate $d_{m}$.
Then we obtain $D$ warped features
${\{{\mathbf{F}}_{d_{m}}^{j\rightarrow i}\}}_{m = 1}^{D}$ that
correspond to feature ${\mathbf{F}}^{i}$. We then measure their feature
correlations with the dot-product operation \[[[63]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib63){.ltx_ref},
[[9]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}\].
The cost volume
${\mathbf{C}}_{i} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{\frac{H}{s} \times \frac{W}{s} \times D}$
for image $i$ is obtained by stacking all correlations. Accordingly, we
obtain cost volumes ${\{{\mathbf{C}}_{i}\}}_{i = 1}^{N}$ for all input
images ${\{{\mathbf{I}}_{i}\}}_{i = 1}^{N}$. For more than two input
views, similar to the strategy in cross-view attention computation, we
select the top-$2$ nearest views for each reference view and compute
feature correlations with only the selected views. This enables our cost
volume construction to achieve a good speed-accuracy trade-off and scale
efficiently to a larger number of input views. The correlation values
for the two selected views are combined with averaging.

### [3.2 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}Monocular Depth Feature Extraction {#monocular-depth-feature-extraction .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S3.SS2.p1 .ltx_para}
Despite the remarkable progress in multi-view feature matching-based
depth estimation \[[[71]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib71){.ltx_ref},
[[57]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib57){.ltx_ref},
[[65]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib65){.ltx_ref}\]
and Gaussian splatting \[[[9]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}\],
they inherently suffer from limitations in challenging situations like
occlusions, texture-less regions, and reflective surfaces. Thus, we
propose to integrate pre-trained monocular depth features into the cost
volume to handle scenarios that are challenging or impossible to match.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S3.SS2.p2 .ltx_para}
More specifically, we leverage the pre-trained monocular depth backbone
from the recent Depth Anything V2 \[[[70]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib70){.ltx_ref}\]
model thanks to its impressive performance on diverse in-the-wild data.
The monocular backbone is a ViT \[[[16]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib16){.ltx_ref},
[[37]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib37){.ltx_ref}\]
model, which has a patch size of 14 and outputs a feature map that is
$1/14$ spatial resolution of the original image. We simply bilinearly
interpolate the spatial resolution of the monocular features to the same
resolution as the cost volume in [[Sec.]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag} [3.1]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#S3.SS1 "3.1 Multi-View Feature Matching ‣ 3 DepthSplat ‣ DepthSplat: Connecting Gaussian Splatting and Depth"){.ltx_ref}
and obtain the monocular feature
${\mathbf{F}}_{mono}^{i} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{\frac{H}{s} \times \frac{W}{s} \times C_{mono}}$
for input image ${\mathbf{I}}_{i}$, where $C_{mono}$ is the dimension of
the monocular feature. This process is performed for all input images in
parallel and we obtain monocular features
${\{{{\mathbf{F}}_{mono}^{i} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{\frac{H}{s} \times \frac{W}{s} \times C_{mono}}}\}}_{i = 1}^{N}$,
which are subsequently used for per-view depth map estimations.

### [3.3 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}Feature Fusion and Depth Regression {#feature-fusion-and-depth-regression .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S3.SS3.p1 .ltx_para}
To achieve robust and multi-view consistent depth predictions, we
combine the monocular feature
${\mathbf{F}}_{mono}^{i} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{\frac{H}{s} \times \frac{W}{s} \times C_{mono}}$
and cost volume
${\mathbf{C}}_{i} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{\frac{H}{s} \times \frac{W}{s} \times D}$
via simple concatenation in the channel dimension. A subsequent 2D
U-Net \[[[42]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib42){.ltx_ref},
[[43]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib43){.ltx_ref}\]
is used to regress depth from the concatenated monocular features and
cost volumes. This process is performed for all the input images in
parallel and for each image, it outputs a tensor of shape
$\frac{H}{s} \times \frac{W}{s} \times D$, where $D$ is the number of
depth candidates. We normalize the $D$ dimension with the softmax
operation and perform a weighted average of all depth candidates to
obtain the depth output.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S3.SS3.p2 .ltx_para}
We also apply a hierarchical matching \[[[25]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib25){.ltx_ref}\]
architecture where an additional refinement step at $2 \times$ higher
feature resolution is employed to improve the performance further. More
specifically, based on the coarse depth prediction, we perform a
correspondence search on the $2 \times$ higher feature maps within the
neighbors of the $2 \times$ upsampled coarse depth prediction. Since we
already have a coarse depth prediction, we only need to search a smaller
range at the higher resolution, and thus, we construct a smaller cost
volume compared to the coarse stage. Such a $2$-scale hierarchical
architecture not only leads to improved efficiency since most
computation is spent on low resolution, but also leads to better results
thanks to the use of higher-resolution features \[[[25]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib25){.ltx_ref}\].
Similar feature fusion and depth regression procedure is used to get
higher-resolution depth predictions, which are subsequently upsampled
with a DPT head \[[[41]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib41){.ltx_ref}\]
to the full resolution.

### [4.1 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}Model Variants {#model-variants .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S4.SS1.p1 .ltx_para}
We first study several different model variants for both depth
estimation and view synthesis tasks. In particular, we explore different
monocular backbones \[[[70]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib70){.ltx_ref}\]
(ViT-S, ViT-B, ViT-L) and different multi-view models ($1$-scale and
$2$-scale). We conduct depth experiments on the large-scale
TartanAir \[[[58]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib58){.ltx_ref}\]
synthetic dataset, which features both indoor and outdoor scenes and has
perfect ground truth depth. The Gaussian splatting experiments are
conducted on the standard RealEstate10K \[[[82]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib82){.ltx_ref}\]
dataset. Following community standards, we report the depth evaluation
metrics \[[[20]{.ltx_text

### [4.3 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}Unsupervised Depth Pre-Training with Gaussian Splatting {#unsupervised-depth-pre-training-with-gaussian-splatting .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S4.SS3.p1 .ltx_para}
By connecting Gaussian splatting and depth, our DepthSplat provides a
way to pre-train the depth model in a fully unsupervised manner. In
particular, we first train our full model on the large-scale multi-view
posed RealEstate10K dataset (contains $\sim$ 67K Youtube videos) with
only the Gaussian splatting rendering loss ([[Eq.]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag} [2]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#S3.E2 "In 3.5 Training Loss ‣ 3 DepthSplat ‣ DepthSplat: Connecting Gaussian Splatting and Depth"){.ltx_ref}),
without any direct supervision on the depth predictions. After
pre-training, we take the pre-trained depth model and further fine-tune
it to the depth task on the mixed TartanAir \[[[58]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib58){.ltx_ref}\]
and VKITTI2 \[[[5]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib5){.ltx_ref}\]
datasets with ground truth depth supervision. In [[Tab.]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag} [4]{.ltx_text
.ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#S4.T4 "In 4.2 Ablation and Analysis ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ DepthSplat: Connecting Gaussian Splatting and Depth"){.ltx_ref},
we evaluate the performance on both in-domain TartanAir test set and the
zero-shot generalization on unseen ScanNet and KITTI datasets. We can
observe that the benefit of pre-training is especially significant on
the challenging datasets like TartanAir and KITTI. The results align
well with the observation in \[[[21]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib21){.ltx_ref}\]
which suggests that the pre-training acts as regularization and guides
the learning towards better minima and enables better generalization to
out-of-distribution datasets. The visual comparison results are provided
in the appendix. Given the increasing popularity of view
synthesis \[[[81]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib81){.ltx_ref},
[[61]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib61){.ltx_ref}\]
and multi-view generative models \[[[45]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib45){.ltx_ref},
[[3]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib3){.ltx_ref}\],
new multi-view datasets \[[[32]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib32){.ltx_ref}\]
and models \[[[55]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib55){.ltx_ref}\]
are gradually introduced, our approach provides a way to pre-train depth
models on large-scale multi-view posed image datasets.

[5 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}Conclusion {#conclusion .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}
-----------------------------------------

Report issue for preceding element

::: {#S5.p1 .ltx_para}
In this paper, we introduce DepthSplat, a new approach to connecting
Gaussian splatting and depth to achieve state-of-the-art results on
ScanNet, RealEstate10K and DL3DV datasets for both depth and view
synthesis tasks. We show that our model enables unsupervised depth
pre-training with Gaussian splatting rendering loss, providing a way to
leverage large-scale posed multi-view image datasets for training more
robust and generalizable multi-view depth models.

Report issue for preceding element
:::

::: {#S5.p2 .ltx_para .ltx_noindent}
[Limitations]{#S5.p2.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_bold}. Our current model
requires camera poses as input along with the multi-view images, which
might be challenging to obtain when the input views are extremely
sparse. An interesting future direction is to remove this requiement by
exploring pose-free models \[[[72]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib72){.ltx_ref},
[[80]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib80){.ltx_ref}\].
In addition, our model predicts pixel-aligned Gaussians, where the
number of Gaussians will be significantly large when processing a large
number of input views \[[[60]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib60){.ltx_ref},
[[79]{.ltx_text
style="font-size:90%;"}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862v3#bib.bib79){.ltx_ref}\].
Further improving the geometry representation and the scalability to
many input views would be exciting future work.
